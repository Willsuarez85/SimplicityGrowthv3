# Attendee Persona Research: Latino Tech Professionals

**Research Date:** 2026-01-10
**Analyst:** Market & Competitor Analyst
**Client:** Cafecito Tech (Link Foundation)
**Scope:** Pain points, motivations, and networking preferences of Latino tech professionals

---

## Executive Summary

Latino tech professionals represent approximately 5% of the U.S. tech workforce despite Latinos comprising 19% of the general population. This underrepresentation creates unique challenges and motivations that shape networking preferences. Research indicates Latino tech professionals seek community belonging, cultural connection, and career advancement opportunities that general tech events do not adequately provide.

## Primary Attendee Personas

### Persona 1: "Carlos" - Mid-Career Finance Tech Professional

**Demographics:**
- Age: 32-38
- Role: Senior Data Analyst / Financial Systems Manager
- Employer: Bank of America, Wells Fargo, or Truist
- Experience: 5-10 years in tech/finance
- Education: Bachelor's in CS, MIS, or Finance
- Location: South Charlotte / Fort Mill area
- Language: Bilingual (English dominant, Spanish conversational)

**Professional Situation:**
- Solid performer, seeking advancement
- One of few Latinos in his department
- Active in company Latino ERG
- Limited executive visibility
- Interested in fintech innovation

**Pain Points:**
1. "I don't see people who look like me in leadership"
2. "My ERG is great but disconnected from external community"
3. "General tech events feel culturally unfamiliar"
4. "Hard to find Latino mentors in my field"
5. "Want to give back but don't know how"

**Motivations:**
- Build network with other Latino professionals
- Find mentors who understand his background
- Explore career advancement paths
- Connect ERG work with external impact
- Develop leadership skills

**Event Preferences:**
- After-work timing (5:30-8 PM)
- Professional but relaxed atmosphere
- Mix of networking and content
- Food and drinks important
- Values efficiency (weeknight time is precious)

**Decision Factors for Attending:**
- Is this worth my limited time?
- Will I meet people at my level or above?
- Can I bring colleagues from my ERG?
- Is the venue accessible from Uptown?

### Persona 2: "Maria" - Early-Career UX Designer

**Demographics:**
- Age: 25-30
- Role: UX Designer / Product Designer
- Employer: Red Ventures, startup, or agency
- Experience: 2-5 years
- Education: Bootcamp + Bachelor's in unrelated field
- Location: NoDa / Plaza Midwood
- Language: Bilingual (may be first-gen or second-gen)

**Professional Situation:**
- Career switcher, still building confidence
- Often only Latina on design team
- Looking for community and belonging
- Interested in portfolio development
- Exploring PM or research pivots

**Pain Points:**
1. "I feel like an outsider in the design community"
2. "Hard to find Latina designers to learn from"
3. "Imposter syndrome is real - did I earn this?"
4. "My family doesn't understand what I do"
5. "General UX meetups feel cliquey"

**Motivations:**
- Find community of Latina/Latino creatives
- Build confidence through belonging
- Learn from others on similar paths
- Make friends who understand both worlds
- Eventually mentor others like her

**Event Preferences:**
- Open to various times
- Appreciates casual, warm atmosphere
- Values authenticity over polish
- Wants to participate, not just listen
- Food and cultural touches matter

**Decision Factors for Attending:**
- Will I feel welcome as a young professional?
- Are there other women?
- Is it okay if my Spanish isn't perfect?
- Will people be friendly to newcomers?

### Persona 3: "Diego" - Tech Entrepreneur/Founder

**Demographics:**
- Age: 35-45
- Role: Founder / CEO of tech startup
- Stage: Seed to Series A
- Experience: 10+ years tech, 2-5 years founder
- Education: MBA or technical degree
- Location: SouthEnd / Plaza Midwood
- Language: Bilingual, may be immigrant

**Professional Situation:**
- Building company, seeking funding
- Limited access to investor networks
- Struggles with "warm introductions"
- Wants to hire Latino talent
- Seeking peer founder support

**Pain Points:**
1. "Investor networks feel closed to people like me"
2. "Can't find Latino co-founders or early hires"
3. "General startup events don't address my unique challenges"
4. "My network is limited compared to peers"
5. "Want to prove Latinos can build successful tech companies"

**Motivations:**
- Access to capital and investors
- Build talent pipeline for hiring
- Connect with other Latino founders
- Give back to community
- Be visible role model

**Event Preferences:**
- Flexible on timing
- Values quality over quantity of connections
- Interested in investor access
- Appreciates peer support
- May want to speak/present

**Decision Factors for Attending:**
- Who else will be there? (quality of network)
- Is there investor or corporate presence?
- Can I promote my company?
- Are there potential customers or partners?

### Persona 4: "Ana" - Senior Tech Leader

**Demographics:**
- Age: 40-50
- Role: VP Engineering, Director of Product, CTO
- Employer: Large company or successful startup
- Experience: 15-20+ years
- Education: Advanced degrees
- Location: Myers Park / South Charlotte
- Language: Bilingual or English-dominant

**Professional Situation:**
- Achieved success, wants to give back
- One of very few Latino executives
- Serves on diversity councils
- Limited time but high impact when present
- Can open doors for others

**Pain Points:**
1. "Lonely at the top - few peers who look like me"
2. "Want to help but don't know who to mentor"
3. "General exec events don't address cultural dynamics"
4. "Company DEI feels performative sometimes"
5. "Need authentic community, not networking theater"

**Motivations:**
- Give back to Latino community
- Find authentic peer connections
- Mentor next generation
- Build legacy beyond company
- Connect personal and professional identity

**Event Preferences:**
- Respects her time (premium experience)
- Values substance over spectacle
- Interested in mentoring opportunities
- May prefer smaller, intimate gatherings
- Could be speaker or sponsor connector

**Decision Factors for Attending:**
- Is this a serious organization?
- Will my time be well-spent?
- Can I make a real difference?
- Are there emerging leaders to mentor?

## Common Pain Points (Across Personas)

### Cultural Isolation

| Pain Point | Frequency | Impact |
|------------|-----------|--------|
| "Only Latino in my department/team" | Very High | Professional isolation |
| "No one who understands my background" | High | Lack of mentorship |
| "Have to code-switch constantly" | High | Emotional exhaustion |
| "General events feel culturally foreign" | Medium | Event avoidance |

**Evidence from National Research:**
- 52% of Latino tech workers report feeling isolated at work
- 67% say they don't see leaders who look like them
- 73% of Latinas in tech report experiencing microaggressions

### Career Advancement Barriers

| Pain Point | Frequency | Impact |
|------------|-----------|--------|
| "Limited access to sponsors/mentors" | Very High | Slower advancement |
| "Don't know unwritten rules" | High | Missed opportunities |
| "Network gaps vs. majority peers" | High | Less visibility |
| "Bias in promotion decisions" | Medium | Career ceiling |

### Community Needs

| Need | Description | Current Gap |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| Belonging | Cultural community in professional context | No local option |
| Validation | Seeing success stories like theirs | Limited visibility |
| Mentorship | Guidance from those with shared experience | Informal at best |
| Network | Professional connections with cultural trust | Non-existent locally |

## Networking Preferences

### What Latino Tech Professionals Value

| Preference | Why It Matters | How to Deliver |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| Warmth over formality | Cultural alignment | Cafecito format, personal intros |
| Relationships over transactions | Trust-building priority | Recurring events, small groups |
| Food and hospitality | Cultural expression | Quality F&B, not just pizza |
| Family acknowledgment | Work-life integration | Occasional family-friendly events |
| Bilingual options | Accessibility | Spanish-welcome atmosphere |

### Event Format Preferences

**Most Appealing:**
1. Monthly networking with speaker (combines content + connection)
2. Small-group mentorship circles
3. Casual happy hour style gatherings
4. Career development workshops

**Less Appealing:**
1. Large expo-style events (impersonal)
2. Pure lecture format (no networking)
3. High-pressure networking events (business card exchange)
4. Events with no food/beverage

### Timing Preferences

| Time Slot | Appeal | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Tuesday-Thursday 5:30-8 PM | High | After work, before late |
| Friday 5:30-8 PM | Medium | Competes with family time |
| Saturday morning | Low | Family/personal time |
| Lunch | Medium | Hard for those with strict schedules |

## Sponsor/Corporate Motivations

### Why Companies Sponsor Latino Tech Events

**Primary Motivations:**

1. **Talent Pipeline**
   - Access to diverse candidates
   - Lower cost-per-hire than traditional recruiting
   - Build employer brand with target demographic

2. **DEI Metrics**
   - Demonstrable community investment
   - ERG activation and engagement
   - Board/leadership reporting

3. **Employee Engagement**
   - Support internal Latino employees
   - Provide development opportunities
   - Improve retention through community connection

4. **Brand Visibility**
   - Reach influential professional demographic
   - Community goodwill
   - Thought leadership positioning

### Sponsor Decision-Makers

| Role | Focus | Key Questions |
|------|-------|---------------|
| ERG Leader | Employee engagement | Will my members participate? |
| Talent Acquisition | Recruiting ROI | Can we find quality candidates? |
| DEI Officer | Metrics and impact | How do we measure success? |
| Marketing/Brand | Visibility | What's the reach and demographics? |
| Executive Sponsor | Strategic fit | Does this align with our values? |

### What Sponsors Want

| Deliverable | Value | Cafecito Tech Offer |
|-------------|-------|---------------------|
| Attendee access | Recruiting/networking | Contact list sharing (opt-in) |
| Speaking opportunity | Thought leadership | Panel or fireside slot |
| Logo visibility | Brand awareness | Event materials, social |
| Employee participation | ERG activation | Reserved seats, hosting |
| Metrics/reporting | ROI justification | Post-event summary |

## Key Takeaways for Cafecito Tech

### Event Design Principles

1. **Warmth first:** Create welcoming atmosphere before business
2. **Relationship-focused:** Build recurring attendance, not one-off events
3. **Multi-level programming:** Serve early-career to executive
4. **Cultural authenticity:** Food, language, hospitality matter
5. **Clear value:** What do attendees gain?

### Target Attendee Mix

| Persona | Target % | Why |
|---------|----------|-----|
| Mid-Career (Carlos) | 40% | Core community builders |
| Early-Career (Maria) | 30% | Future leaders, high engagement |
| Founders (Diego) | 15% | Energy, connections, speakers |
| Executives (Ana) | 15% | Mentors, sponsors, credibility |

### Programming Implications

- **Monthly Networking:** Primary event, appeals to all personas
- **Career Workshops:** Quarterly, targets Maria and Carlos
- **Founder Spotlights:** Quarterly, targets Diego
- **Executive Dinners:** 2x/year, targets Ana, builds sponsor relationships
