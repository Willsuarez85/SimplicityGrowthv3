# Visual Identity Direction: Cafecito Tech

**Client:** Cafecito Tech (Link Foundation - Community InLink Program)
**Document Type:** Visual Mood & Design Direction
**Purpose:** Guide visual expression across all event materials and platforms
**Version:** 1.0

---

## Visual Identity Philosophy

Cafecito Tech's visual identity must balance two worlds simultaneously: professional tech credibility and authentic Latino cultural warmth. We are neither a corporate tech conference nor a cultural festival—we are the bridge between these worlds.

**Guiding Principle:**
*Visuals should feel like walking into a friend's well-designed home for a dinner party, not a sterile conference room or a themed restaurant.*

---

## Primary Visual Mood

### Confident Clarity

**What This Feels Like:**
Clean, organized, professional—but with warmth. Think Apple Store meets cafetería moderna. High-quality execution that respects attendees' time and intelligence, infused with cultural authenticity that doesn't perform or apologize.

**Visual Descriptors:**
- **Modern:** Contemporary design, not dated or overly traditional
- **Warm:** Inviting colors, natural lighting, human moments
- **Authentic:** Real people, real moments, real connections
- **Professional:** Quality execution, attention to detail, credibility

**NOT:**
- Cluttered or chaotic
- Sterile or corporate cold
- Stereotypical or costume-y
- Amateur or low-quality

---

### Secondary Mood: Cultural Warmth

**What This Feels Like:**
Latino cultural elements integrated naturally—not as decoration, but as lived reality. The visual equivalent of code-switching: professional foundation with cultural accents.

**Visual Descriptors:**
- **Hospitable:** Warmth conveyed through color, composition, faces
- **Vibrant:** Energy without overwhelming
- **Communal:** Group moments, connection, togetherness
- **Pride:** Cultural elements celebrated, never minimized

## Typography Direction

### Primary Typeface Philosophy

**Bilingual-Friendly, Modern, Approachable**

We need typefaces that:
1. Handle Spanish characters well (accents, ñ, ¿, ¡)
2. Read clearly on screens and printed materials
3. Feel contemporary, not dated
4. Balance professionalism with warmth

### Recommended Type Direction

**Headlines & Titles:**
- **Style:** Sans-serif, medium-heavy weight, geometric or humanist
- **Examples:** Montserrat Bold, Proxima Nova Semibold, Inter Bold
- **Why:** Clean, modern, confident without being aggressive
- **Character:** Professional but approachable, tech-credible

**Body Text:**
- **Style:** Sans-serif, regular weight, high readability
- **Examples:** Open Sans, Lato, Source Sans Pro
- **Why:** Maximum readability across digital platforms
- **Character:** Neutral, accessible, human

**Spanish Accent Usage:**
- Always include proper accents (café not cafe, José not Jose)
- Test typefaces with full Spanish character set before selection
- Ensure ñ, á, é, í, ó, ú render properly at all sizes

### Typography in Practice

**Name Tags:**
- First Name: Large, bold (Montserrat Bold, 48pt+)
- Company • Role: Medium, regular (Open Sans Regular, 24pt)
- Cultural Note: Pronounceable names (include phonetic if needed)

**Social Media Graphics:**
- Headlines: Bold, short, impactful
- Body: Minimal text, high readability
- Spanish phrases: Same typeface, same weight (integrated, not separate)

**Presentation Slides:**
- Headers: Bold sans-serif
- Bullet points: Regular sans-serif
- Maximum 5-7 words per line for bilingual audiences

## Photography Style

### Core Photography Philosophy

**Authentic Humanity Over Polished Perfection**

Cafecito Tech photography should feel like your friend with a good camera captured genuine moments, not a professional photoshoot with models.

### Primary Photography Style: Candid Connection

**Characteristics:**
- **Real attendees, real moments** (not stock photos or staged shots)
- **Natural expressions** (genuine smiles, focused listening, animated conversation)
- **Environmental context** (venue visible, food present, event atmosphere)
- **Cultural diversity visible** (range of skin tones, ages, styles)
- **Connection prioritized** (people engaging with each other, not posing alone)

**Lighting:**
- Natural light preferred when possible
- Warm artificial lighting (2700-4000K) for evening events
- Avoid harsh overhead fluorescent
- Golden hour outdoor shots when available

**Composition:**
- Medium shots (waist-up or closer) prioritized over wide room shots
- Rule of thirds for dynamic interest
- Negative space balanced with activity
- Foreground/background depth (people in conversation, others visible behind)

### Subject Matter Priorities

**Primary Subjects (60% of photography):**
1. **Two or three people in conversation** - Shows connection, community
2. **Speaker with engaged audience** - Shows content + community blend
3. **Food and hospitality moments** - Shows care, cultural authenticity
4. **Introductions happening** - Shows Connector archetype in action

**Secondary Subjects (30% of photography):**
1. **Wider venue shots** (with people, not empty rooms)
2. **Cultural food details** (pan dulce close-up, café de olla steaming)
3. **Name tag close-ups** (shows diversity of companies/roles)
4. **Candid reactions** (laughing, nodding, thinking)

**Tertiary Subjects (10% of photography):**
1. **Venue details** (signage, branding)
2. **Setup moments** (pre-event atmosphere)
3. **Group shots** (end of event, if requested)

### Photography Examples: On-Brand vs Off-Brand

✅ **On-Brand Photography:**
- Two professionals (one in blazer, one in tech casual) laughing mid-conversation, warm lighting, venue visible in background
- Speaker gesturing while presenting, 3-4 attendees visible in foreground listening intently, natural expressions
- Close-up of hands holding coffee cup next to plate of pan dulce, conversation happening in soft-focus background
- Small group of 4-5 people in circle, animated discussion, diverse skin tones and ages, warm venue lighting

❌ **Off-Brand Photography:**
- Corporate headshot style: single person staring at camera, neutral background, forced smile
- Stock photo: models in business attire shaking hands, staged, too polished
- Stereotype: mariachi hats, sombreros, Day of the Dead makeup (cultural costume, not authentic community)
- Empty room: venue setup with no people (lacks community energy)
- Overly filtered: Instagram filters, unnatural color grading, over-saturated

### Photography Technical Specs

| Use Case | Aspect Ratio | Resolution | Style |
|----------|--------------|------------|-------|
| LinkedIn carousel | 1:1 square | 1080x1080 | Candid, professional lighting |
| Instagram feed | 4:5 portrait | 1080x1350 | Warm, vibrant, cultural moments |
| Instagram stories | 9:16 vertical | 1080x1920 | Quick moments, energetic |
| Event recap blog | 16:9 landscape | 1920x1080 | Wider shots, venue context |
| Website hero | 16:9 landscape | 2400x1350 | High-quality, representative |

### Cultural Authenticity in Photography

**Capture Naturally:**
- Bilingual conversations (people code-switching mid-sentence is authentic)
- Cultural food being enjoyed (not just displayed)
- Range of professional styles (from tech casual to finance business)
- Genuine emotion (pride, joy, concentration, curiosity)

**Avoid:**
- All imagery focused on food/stereotypes
- Everyone in same clothing style (shows lack of diversity)
- Overly formal corporate posing
- Cultural elements as props rather than lived reality

## Event Materials & Physical Branding

### Name Tags (Critical First Impression)

**Design:**
- **Size:** 4" x 3" badge
- **Background:** Cream (#F5F1E8) with Terracotta accent bar
- **First Name:** 48pt Montserrat Bold, Deep Teal (#1B5560)
- **Company • Role:** 24pt Open Sans Regular, Slate Gray (#515859)
- **Logo:** Small Cafecito Tech logo, bottom right corner

**Why This Matters:**
Name tags are the first visual touchpoint. Large first names signal "we want to know YOU," not "here's my sales pitch."

**Example Layout:**
```
┌─────────────────────────────┐
│  CARLOS                     │ ← 48pt Bold, Deep Teal
│  Bank of America • Analyst  │ ← 24pt Regular, Slate Gray
│                             │
│           [small logo]      │ ← Bottom right
└─────────────────────────────┘
  [Terracotta accent bar]
```

### Event Signage

**Venue Entry Sign:**
- Large "Cafecito Tech" in Montserrat Bold
- "Bienvenidos • Welcome" in Open Sans Regular
- Date + event theme
- Terracotta + Deep Teal color scheme
- Clean, readable from 10+ feet away

**Directional Signage:**
- Icons + text (universal understanding)
- Bilingual when space allows
- High contrast for readability
- Consistent with color palette

**Table Signage:**
- Food labels bilingual: "Pan Dulce • Sweet Bread"
- Sponsor table: Company logo + "We're Hiring!"
- Registration table: Clear, welcoming

### Table/Venue Styling

**Tablecloths & Linens:**
- Cream or white (neutral, clean)
- Avoid: Bright red/green (too literal)
- Accent with terracotta napkins or centerpieces

**Centerpieces (if used):**
- Simple, modern: succulent plants, simple vases
- Cultural nods: ceramic pieces in terracotta tones
- Avoid: Stereotypical (sombreros, piñatas, flags)

**Lighting:**
- Warm white (2700-3200K) preferred
- String lights if available (adds warmth)
- Avoid: Harsh overhead fluorescent

## Platform-Specific Visual Adaptations

### LinkedIn (Professional Action)

**Visual Priorities:**
1. Clean, professional execution
2. Data visualization when relevant (attendance growth, job placements)
3. Professional photography (business casual to business formal)
4. Terracotta + Deep Teal color dominance
5. Minimal Spanish text (1-2 phrases max)

**Avoid on LinkedIn:**
- Overly casual imagery
- Too much Spanish (audience may be English-dominant)
- Low-quality photos
- Memes or humor

### Instagram (Cultural Connection)

**Visual Priorities:**
1. Vibrant, warm color palette (Terracotta + Sunlit Gold)
2. Cultural food and hospitality moments
3. Candid, joyful expressions
4. Stories > Feed for real-time moments
5. UGC reposting (4x higher engagement)

**Avoid on Instagram:**
- Corporate stock photos
- All text, no imagery
- Sterile professional headshots
- Ignoring Stories feature (high engagement)

### WhatsApp/Slack (Community Management)

**Visual Priorities:**
1. Simple, quick-loading images
2. Event logistics graphics (maps, parking info)
3. Emoji use for warmth (but not excessive)
4. Job posting graphics (simple template)

**Avoid in WhatsApp:**
- Large file sizes (slow loading)
- Overly designed graphics (simple better)
- Too frequent visual messages (text primary here)

## Cultural Visual Elements (Integration, Not Decoration)

### Authentic Cultural Integration

**DO: Integrate Naturally**
- Terracotta clay tones (cultural color reference, not literal)
- Geometric patterns inspired by textiles (subtle, modern interpretation)
- Coffee/cafecito imagery (relevant to event name, culturally resonant)
- Real Latino professionals in photography (lived diversity)
- Bilingual text as integrated design element

**DON'T: Decorate Stereotypically**
- ❌ Sombreros, maracas, piñatas
- ❌ Mexican flag colors dominating
- ❌ Day of the Dead imagery (unless event-specific)
- ❌ Cartoon chili peppers or cacti
- ❌ "Fiesta" aesthetic

### Modern Latino Visual Language

**What This Looks Like:**
- Clean design + warm color palette = modern + cultural
- Professional photography + pan dulce in frame = integrated authenticity
- Sans-serif type + Spanish accents = bilingual modernity
- Geometric patterns (inspired by textiles) + minimal application = cultural nod without costume

**Example:**
A LinkedIn carousel with:
- Clean Deep Teal background
- Professional headshot of speaker
- Subtle geometric border pattern (textile-inspired, Terracotta)
- Headline in English, tagline "Nos vemos Thursday" in Spanish
- Result: Professional, modern, culturally authentic

### ✅ On-Brand Visual Examples

**Example 1: Event Announcement Post**

- Photography: Professional photo of Ana (VP Engineering)
- Background: Deep Teal
- Text overlay: "Meet Ana Rodriguez • VP Engineering @ [Company]"
- Quote: "From junior developer to VP—here's how"

- Blurry smartphone photo
- WordArt-style text

## Video & Motion Graphics Direction

### Event Recap Video Style

**Length:** 60-90 seconds

**Structure:**
1. Opening (5 sec): Venue, people arriving, warm atmosphere
2. Highlights (45 sec): Conversations, speaker moments, food, laughter
3. Closing (10 sec): "Nos vemos next month" + date

**Visual Treatment:**
- Natural color grading (warm, not over-saturated)
- Upbeat background music (modern, energetic, not stereotypical mariachi)
- Minimal text overlays (let visuals tell story)
- Fast cuts (3-5 seconds per clip) for energy

**Avoid:**
- Overly slow, corporate pace
- Heavy filters or effects
- Lengthy talking head segments
- No music or wrong music choice

### Animated Graphics (Social Media)

**Style:**
- Simple, clean animations
- Terracotta + Deep Teal color palette
- Geometric shapes (modern, not stereotypical patterns)
- Text animations: Fade, slide (not bounce or spin)

**Use Cases:**
- Event countdown graphics
- Registration reminders
- Quote graphics (animated text reveal)

## Accessibility Considerations

### Color Accessibility

**Contrast Ratios (WCAG AA Compliance):**
- Deep Teal (#1B5560) on Cream (#F5F1E8): 7.2:1 ✅
- Terracotta (#D4754E) on Cream: 3.8:1 ⚠️ (use for accent, not body text)
- Slate Gray (#515859) on White: 8.1:1 ✅

**Best Practices:**
- Body text always high contrast (Slate Gray or Deep Teal on light backgrounds)
- Avoid text on Terracotta backgrounds unless white text
- Test all graphics for colorblind accessibility

### Typography Accessibility

- Minimum body text size: 16pt digital, 12pt print
- Line spacing: 1.5x for readability
- Avoid all-caps for long text (hard to read)
- Ensure Spanish characters render properly on all platforms

- [ ] Real people, real moments (not stock)
