# Brand Scenarios & Contexts: Cafecito Tech

**Client:** Cafecito Tech (Link Foundation - Community InLink Program)
**Document Type:** Contextual Brand Applications
**Purpose:** Define where and how Cafecito Tech shows up across all touchpoints
**Version:** 1.0

---

## Document Overview

This document provides concrete examples of how Cafecito Tech's brand personality, voice, and values manifest across the entire event lifecycle—from pre-event promotion through post-event follow-up, sponsor relationships, and crisis scenarios.

Each scenario includes:
- **Context:** What's happening and why
- **Brand Approach:** How Cafecito Tech responds based on core identity
- **Example Language:** Actual communications demonstrating brand voice
- **Success Indicators:** What good looks like
- **Avoid:** Off-brand responses to watch for

### Scenario 2: First-Timer Personal Outreach (1 Week Out)

**Context:**
Someone registered for their first Cafecito Tech event. They're likely nervous, uncertain what to expect, and may be considering not showing up.

**Brand Approach:**
- Extra warm, reassuring, proactive
- Address unspoken anxiety about fitting in
- Remove logistical friction
- Personal connection (not automated)
- Offer introduction upon arrival

**Example: LinkedIn DM**

```
Hi Maria,

Noticed you registered for Cafecito Tech next Thursday—excited to have you! 🎉

Since it's your first time, here's what to expect:

• Doors open at 6 PM. Most people arrive 6-6:30.
• Grab dinner first (we provide food), then mingle.
• I'll personally introduce you to 2-3 people when you arrive so you're not flying solo.
• Speaker starts at 7 PM (30 min), then back to networking.
• No formal icebreakers—just natural conversations.

Parking: [Specific parking location and cost]
Dietary needs: We have vegan/GF options. Any allergies I should know about?

One more thing: Everyone at their first event asks "Am I tech enough for this?" The answer is yes. If you work in tech or aspire to, you belong here.

See you Thursday. Nos vemos!

William
Cafecito Tech
```

**Success Indicators:**
- 70%+ of first-timers who receive personal outreach actually attend
- Recipients reply with questions (engagement signal)
- First-timers report feeling welcomed before even arriving
- Low no-show rate for personally contacted attendees

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Generic automated email
- ❌ Sales pitch tone
- ❌ No logistical details (parking, food, timing)
- ❌ Formal corporate language

### Scenario 3: Speaker Coordination (2 Weeks Out)

**Context:**
Confirmed speaker needs event details, context on audience, and support to deliver valuable content aligned with community needs.

**Brand Approach:**
- Professional partner (not subordinate asking for favor)
- Clear audience context and expectations
- Collaborative on content
- Grateful without groveling
- Sets speaker up for success

**Example: Email to Speaker**

**Subject:** Cafecito Tech March 28 - Speaker Details & Audience Context

```
Hi Ana,

Thanks again for speaking at Cafecito Tech on March 28! Here's everything you need to prepare:

📅 EVENT LOGISTICS
• Date: Thursday, March 28, 2026
• Your Speaking Time: 7:00-7:30 PM (30 min total: 20 min talk, 10 min Q&A)
• Venue: [Address, parking details, arrival time]
• A/V: We'll have mic, projector, HDMI connection. Bring your laptop.

👥 AUDIENCE CONTEXT
• 40-50 Latino tech professionals (developers, designers, PMs, analysts, founders)
• Career levels: 60% mid-career (3-10 years), 25% early-career, 15% senior/exec
• Primary interest: Career advancement paths, navigating tech as Latino professionals
• What resonates: Real talk about challenges, specific tactical advice, authentic stories

💡 CONTENT SUGGESTION
Your topic "From IC to VP: Navigating Tech Leadership" is perfect. Here's what we'd love:

1. Your career path (5 min) - How you got from individual contributor to VP
2. Key decisions (10 min) - Pivotal moments, what you'd do differently
3. Navigating as Latina (5 min) - Real talk about being one of few in leadership
4. Q&A (10 min) - Audience will have great questions

Feel free to adjust—you know your story best. Just keep it practical and honest. This community values real experience over polished perfection.

📸 POST-EVENT
We'll share photos and key takeaways on LinkedIn (with your approval). If you have a specific message or project to promote, let me know and we'll include it.

Questions? Call/text me: [phone]

Thank you for investing in this community. It matters.

Nos vemos,
William
```

**Success Indicators:**
- Speaker feels prepared and valued
- Content aligns with audience needs
- Speaker promotes event to their network
- Q&A engagement is high (8+ questions)
- Post-event speaker feedback is positive

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Last-minute details
- ❌ Unclear audience expectations
- ❌ "We're so honored you'd speak for us" (groveling)
- ❌ No follow-up after commitment

## During-Event Scenarios

### Scenario 5: Door Greeting & First-Timer Welcome

**Context:**
Attendees are arriving. Some are first-timers who are nervous. Others are returning community members. Host needs to set warm, inclusive tone immediately.

**Brand Approach:**
- Personal greeting for everyone
- Extra attention to first-timers
- Immediate introduction facilitation
- Warm energy without overwhelming
- Logistical clarity (food, bathrooms, timing)

**Example: Door Greeting Script**

**Returning Attendee:**
```
"Carlos! Good to see you again. How's the new role at Truist going?"

[Listen to response]

"That's great. Hey, there's someone I want you to meet—Diego just raised a seed round for his fintech startup. You two should talk. Let me grab him."

[Facilitate introduction, then move to next arrival]
```

**First-Timer:**
```
"Hi! Welcome to Cafecito Tech. First time?"

[Attendee confirms]

"Perfect. I'm William. Grab some food first, then I'll introduce you to a few people. What do you do?"

[Attendee shares: "UX designer at Red Ventures"]

"Awesome. We have three other designers here tonight—I'll connect you. And Ana, our speaker, used to lead design at Wells Fargo. You'll love her talk."

[Point to food, bathrooms, seating]

"No pressure—just grab food, find a spot, and I'll come find you in about 10 minutes to make intros. Sound good?"

[Make note of name, return within 10 minutes as promised]
```

**First-Timer Feeling Anxious:**
```
"Hi! First time? Perfect. Come with me—let me introduce you to Maria. She was also new last month and now she's basically running the place."

[Walk them directly to another friendly face]

"Maria, this is Sofia—also a UX designer, also first time. Sofia, Maria is at Amazon. You two will have plenty to talk about."

[Stay for 30 seconds to ensure conversation starts, then move on]
```

**Success Indicators:**
- Every attendee greeted within 2 minutes of arrival
- First-timers introduced to 2-3 people within first 15 minutes
- No one standing alone uncomfortably
- Energy feels welcoming, not chaotic

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Ignoring arrivals while talking to friends
- ❌ Generic "Help yourself to food" without personal touch
- ❌ Overwhelming first-timers with too many intros at once
- ❌ Forgetting promised introductions

### Scenario 6: Speaker Introduction

**Context:**
Speaker is about to present. Need to transition from networking to content, build speaker credibility, and set audience expectations.

**Brand Approach:**
- Warm but brief (respect time)
- Humanize speaker (not just credentials)
- Connect speaker to community values
- Set clear expectations for format
- Confident introduction (not apologetic)

**Example: Speaker Introduction (Live)**

```
"Alright everyone, let's come together. Grab your seat, bring your cafecito.

I want to introduce tonight's speaker, Ana Rodríguez.

Ana is VP of Engineering at Wells Fargo. She leads a team of 80+ engineers building the next generation of digital banking tools. That's the LinkedIn version.

Here's what matters to us: Ana started her career as a junior developer at a startup. She was often the only Latina in the room—sound familiar? Over 15 years, she navigated pivots, promotions, and plenty of moments where she questioned if she belonged.

Now she's a VP. And she's here to share how she did it—the real version. Not the polished conference talk. The honest one.

We have 30 minutes: Ana will talk for 20, then we'll open it up for Q&A. She's here for real questions, so don't hold back.

Ana, welcome. The floor is yours."

[Applause, step aside]
```

**Success Indicators:**
- Speaker feels valued and introduced well
- Audience understands speaker's credibility and relevance
- Transition from networking to content is smooth
- Q&A session is robust (8+ questions)
- Energy remains warm and engaged

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Reading speaker's entire LinkedIn bio
- ❌ Apologizing for disrupting networking
- ❌ Over-hyping: "The most amazing speaker ever!"
- ❌ Under-introducing: "Here's Ana"

### Scenario 7: Mid-Event Connection Facilitation

**Context:**
During networking time, noticed two people who should meet but haven't connected yet. Opportunity to actively facilitate relationship.

**Brand Approach:**
- Active connector (not passive observer)
- Context-driven introductions
- Brief facilitation, then step away
- Genuine connection points
- Empowers both people

**Example: Active Introduction**

```
[Approach first person]

"Diego, quick question—you mentioned you're raising a seed round for your fintech app, right?"

[Diego confirms]

"Come with me. There's someone you need to meet."

[Walk to second person]

"Ana, this is Diego. Diego's building a fintech app focused on Latino remittances—raised $500K so far, going for seed round. Ana's VP of Engineering at Wells Fargo, and she mentors a ton of early-stage founders.

Diego, Ana led Wells Fargo's digital wallet product launch last year—$20M project. She knows fintech infrastructure inside and out.

You two should talk. I'll leave you to it."

[Step away, let them connect]
```

**Success Indicators:**
- Meaningful connections happen beyond self-introductions
- Attendees report "I met exactly who I needed to meet"
- Follow-up conversations happen post-event
- Community members start facilitating intros themselves

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Generic intros: "You two should meet"
- ❌ Interrupting deep conversations unnecessarily
- ❌ Hovering after introduction
- ❌ Transactional matchmaking (forced networking)

### Scenario 8: In-Event Crisis: Food Runs Out Early

**Context:**
Catering underestimated attendance. Food runs out 45 minutes into event. Late arrivals have no food. Need immediate solution.

**Brand Approach:**
- Acknowledge immediately (don't ignore)
- Solve quickly without drama
- Communicate transparently
- Maintain hospitality focus
- Learn for next time

**Example: In-Person Announcement**

```
"Hey everyone, quick heads up—we underestimated food tonight and we're running low. That's on me.

Here's what we're doing: I just ordered 20 pizzas from [nearby restaurant], they'll be here in 25 minutes. In the meantime, there's still some pan dulce and drinks.

If you just arrived and you're hungry, I'm sorry. Food's on the way, and next month we'll order way more.

Back to networking. Speaker starts in 15 minutes."
```

**Example: Text to Arriving Attendees**

```
"Hi [Name]—heads up, we ran out of main food but more is arriving in 20 min. Pan dulce and drinks still available. Apologies for the oversight!"
```

**Post-Event Follow-Up:**
```
LinkedIn post next day:

"Learning moment from last night: we ran out of food 45 minutes in. That's on us. We ordered emergency pizza, everyone got fed, but it wasn't the hospitality standard Cafecito Tech stands for.

Next month: we're over-ordering. Hospitality matters.

Thanks for your patience. See you in April. 🙏"
```

**Success Indicators:**
- Problem solved within 30 minutes
- No attendees leave hungry or upset
- Transparent communication maintains trust
- Lesson applied to next event (never repeat)

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Ignoring the problem
- ❌ Blaming caterer publicly
- ❌ Defensive response
- ❌ Diminishing issue: "It's not a big deal"

### Scenario 10: First-Timer Follow-Up (48 Hours Post-Event)

**Context:**
First-time attendee came to event. Want to check in, ensure they felt welcomed, and encourage return.

**Brand Approach:**
- Personal (not automated)
- Genuine interest in experience
- Invite feedback
- Facilitate next connection
- Low-pressure return invitation

**Example: LinkedIn DM**

```
Hi Maria,

How was your first Cafecito Tech experience Thursday?

I saw you talking with Ana (our speaker) and Diego (fintech founder) for a while—looked like great conversations. Hope you made some solid connections.

Any feedback for us? What worked? What didn't? We're always learning.

Also, if you're interested in connecting with other UX designers in the community, let me know. I can intro you to 3-4 others who weren't there Thursday.

Next event is April 25. Hope to see you again. 🙌

Nos vemos,
William
```

**If No Response After First Message (7 Days Later):**

```
Hi Maria,

No worries if you've been busy! Just wanted to follow up once more.

If Cafecito Tech wasn't the right fit for you, totally understand. But if timing was just off, next event is April 25.

Either way, wishing you success in your UX career. If I can ever help with intros or connections, let me know.

Best,
William
```

[No further follow-up unless they respond]

**Success Indicators:**
- 60%+ of first-timers respond to follow-up
- 40%+ of first-timers return by Month 3
- First-timers provide constructive feedback
- First-timers join WhatsApp group post-event

**Avoid:**
- ❌ No follow-up at all
- ❌ Automated generic survey
- ❌ Aggressive "Why didn't you respond?" energy
- ❌ Multiple follow-ups (becomes spam)

## Sponsor Communication Scenarios

- Professional peer (not supplicant)

- Mutual benefit framing
- Specific ask

• WhatsApp community with 40-60% engagement

We're seeking a presenting sponsor for our monthly events. In return, Bank of America gains:
• Direct access to diverse tech talent pipeline
• ERG activation opportunity (bring HOLA members to events)
• Brand visibility to 150-300 professionals/quarter
• Community partnership that demonstrates real commitment

Would you be open to a 20-minute call to explore this? I can share more specifics on structure, metrics, and partnership models.

Best,
William Suarez
Founder, Cafecito Tech
Link Foundation - Community InLink Program

**Success Indicators:**
- Response rate of 30%+ from cold outreach
- Meeting scheduled within 2 weeks
- Clear interest in value proposition
- Follow-up conversation happens

**Avoid:**
- ❌ "We'd be honored if you'd consider sponsoring us" (groveling)
- ❌ Vague value proposition
- ❌ No data or credibility signals

### Scenario 18: Attendee Conflict (During Event)

**Context:**
Two attendees get into heated political argument during networking time. Energy in room shifts uncomfortably. Other attendees notice.

**Brand Approach:**
- De-escalate immediately
- Private conversation, not public spectacle
- Maintain community standards
- Refocus on shared purpose
- Follow up if needed

**Example: Immediate Intervention (Quiet, Private)**

```
[Approach two attendees mid-argument]

"Hey guys, can I borrow you both for a second?"

[Walk them away from main group to quiet corner]

"I can tell you're both passionate about this topic, and I respect that. But Cafecito Tech isn't the venue for political debates—we're here to build professional community across all viewpoints.

I need to ask you both to step back from this conversation tonight. If you want to continue offline, that's your call, but not here.

Sound fair?"

[Wait for agreement]

"Great. Let's get back to networking. Diego, you mentioned you're hiring—there are three developers here you should meet. Carlos, Ana's over there and you two were talking about fintech earlier. Let's reconnect you."

[Redirect both to positive interactions]
```

**If Conflict Continues:**

```
[Pull individual aside privately]

"Hey, I appreciate you being here, but I need you to take a break from this conversation. If you can't, I'm going to have to ask you to leave for tonight. This community only works if everyone feels safe and respected.

What do you want to do?"

[If they agree to stop: "Thank you. Let's move forward."]
[If they refuse: "I understand. This event isn't the right fit tonight. Happy to connect offline another time."]
```

**Post-Event Follow-Up (If Needed):**

```
LinkedIn DM next day:

"Hi [Name],

About last night—I had to step in during that conversation because it was shifting the room's energy in a way that wasn't aligned with Cafecito Tech's purpose.

I want everyone to feel welcome, and that includes respecting that people come from different political perspectives. Our shared ground is professional community, career growth, and Latino identity in tech.

If you're open to that, I'd love to have you back. If not, no hard feelings.

Let me know.

Best,
William"
```

**Success Indicators:**
- Conflict de-escalated within 5 minutes
- Room energy returns to normal
- Other attendees feel safe
- Both individuals handled respectfully
- Incident doesn't repeat

**Avoid:**
- ❌ Ignoring conflict and hoping it resolves
- ❌ Public shaming or calling out
- ❌ Taking sides in political debate
- ❌ Letting it escalate to shouting
