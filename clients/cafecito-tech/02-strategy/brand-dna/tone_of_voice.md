# Tone of Voice: Cafecito Tech

**Client:** Cafecito Tech (Link Foundation - Community InLink Program)
**Document Type:** Voice & Language Guidelines
**Purpose:** Usable by humans and AI systems for consistent communication
**Version:** 1.0

## Language Style

### Primary Characteristics

**1. Bilingual (English-Primary with Spanish Cultural Accents)**

**What This Means:**
- All essential information in English (accessibility for 70% of U.S. Latino professionals who prefer English-primary communication)
- Spanish phrases used for cultural warmth and belonging signals
- Code-switching mirrors how audience naturally speaks (2.8x higher engagement than single-language posts)
- Never full translation—Spanish enhances, not replaces

**Examples:**

✅ **On-Brand:**
- "Join us for Cafecito Tech this Thursday! Nos vemos at 6 PM."
- "¿Cafecito catch-up this week? Let's connect before the next event."
- "Congrats on the new role, Carlos! ¡Éxito in your new chapter!"

❌ **Off-Brand:**
- "Únete a Cafecito Tech este jueves a las 6 PM." (full Spanish—excludes English-dominant attendees)
- "Join us for Coffee Tech this Thursday at 6 PM." (removes cultural signaling entirely)

---

**2. Warm and Direct**

**What This Means:**
- Friendly without being overly casual or unprofessional
- Clear and concise without being cold or corporate
- Personal without being invasive
- Inviting without being pushy

**Examples:**

✅ **On-Brand:**
- "First time at Cafecito Tech? Perfect. I'll introduce you to a few people when you arrive."
- "Parking can be tricky—here's the closest lot with free evening spots."
- "Thanks for being part of our community. See you next month!"

❌ **Off-Brand:**
- "Hey fam! Cafecito Tech is gonna be lit! 🔥🔥🔥" (too casual, inappropriate energy)
- "Attendees are advised to arrive promptly at the designated time to maximize networking opportunities." (corporate, cold)
- "You HAVE to come to this event! Don't miss out!!!" (pushy, desperate)

---

**3. Conversational and Accessible**

**What This Means:**
- Speak like a knowledgeable friend, not a corporation
- Use contractions ("we're" not "we are")
- Short sentences when possible
- Avoid jargon unless specifically addressing technical audience (and even then, explain)

**Examples:**

✅ **On-Brand:**
- "We're building the Latino tech community Charlotte needs."
- "Imposter syndrome? Everyone in this room has felt it."
- "Let's grab cafecito before the event starts."

❌ **Off-Brand:**
- "Cafecito Tech facilitates synergistic networking opportunities to leverage diversity within the technology ecosystem." (jargon-heavy, corporate)
- "We are constructing a comprehensive professional development infrastructure." (overly formal)

---

**4. Professional but Human**

**What This Means:**
- We're credible tech professionals, not a social club
- We acknowledge real challenges honestly
- We celebrate wins without hype or exaggeration
- We're confident without being arrogant

**Examples:**

✅ **On-Brand:**
- "Breaking into tech is hard. Breaking in as a Latino professional comes with extra barriers. Let's talk about how to navigate them."
- "Three attendees from last month's event landed new roles this week. That's why we're here."
- "We're not the biggest tech event in Charlotte. We're the only one built for Latino professionals."

❌ **Off-Brand:**
- "Cafecito Tech is THE ULTIMATE networking experience you CAN'T AFFORD TO MISS!" (hyperbolic, salesy)
- "Facing microaggressions in tech? Just be more confident!" (dismissive, tone-deaf)
- "We're a small gathering for Latinos in tech." (underselling, lacks confidence)

## Emotional Posture

### Primary Emotions We Express

**1. Confident Warmth**
- We know who we are and why we matter
- We welcome people in without neediness
- Energy: "We're glad you're here" (not "Please validate us")

**2. Cultural Pride**
- Celebrate Latino identity openly, never apologetically
- Code-switching is strength, not compromise
- Energy: "This is who we are, and it's an asset"

**3. Steady Optimism**
- Forward-looking without toxic positivity
- Acknowledge challenges while maintaining hope
- Energy: "It's hard, and we're making progress together"

**4. Authentic Empathy**
- Understand real pain points (isolation, imposter syndrome, career barriers)
- Validate struggles without dwelling in victimhood
- Energy: "I see you. I've been there. Here's how we move forward."

### Emotional Boundaries

**We Do NOT Express:**
- **Desperation:** Never beg for attendance, sponsorships, or validation
- **Anger:** Not combative, divisive, or bitter (even when addressing injustice)
- **Performative Activism:** No empty virtue signaling or corporate diversity theater
- **Elitism:** Not exclusive or gatekeeping (we're inclusive by design)
- **Passivity:** Not waiting for permission or external validation to exist

## Signature Phrases

### Spanish Cultural Touchpoints (Use Regularly)

| Phrase | Meaning | When to Use |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Cafecito** | Small coffee, casual meetup | Event name, informal gatherings ("cafecito catch-up") |
| **Nos vemos** | See you (literally "we see each other") | Event reminders, closings |
| **¡Éxito!** | Success! Congratulations! | Celebrating wins, new jobs, promotions |
| **Vamos** | Let's go, let's do this | Call to action, momentum building |
| **Bienvenidos** | Welcome | Greeting first-timers, event opening |
| **La comunidad** | The community | Collective framing |
| **Paisanos** | Fellow countrymen/women, peers | Informal community reference (use sparingly) |

**Usage Guidance:**
- Maximum 1-2 Spanish phrases per message (avoid overload)
- Always ensure English context makes Spanish phrase understandable
- Use naturally, not forced ("Join us Thursday—nos vemos at 6!" flows; "Bienvenidos, paisanos, vamos a Cafecito Tech, éxito!" is overload)

### English Signature Expressions

| Expression | When to Use |
|------------|-------------|
| "Tu comunidad tech en Charlotte" | Tagline, bio descriptions |
| "Your people are here" | First-timer messaging, belonging signal |
| "Let me introduce you to..." | Networking facilitation, connector role |
| "We're building this together" | Community ownership framing |
| "Same day, same time, every month" | Consistency messaging |
| "It's hard. Here's how others did it." | Addressing challenges |

## Forbidden Territory

### Words and Phrases to Avoid

**Corporate Jargon:**
❌ "Leverage synergies"
❌ "Thought leadership ecosystem"
❌ "Maximize ROI on networking"
❌ "Strategic alignment initiatives"
❌ "Best-in-class diversity programming"

**Why:** Sounds inauthentic, impersonal, transactional. We're a community, not a corporation.

---

**Overly Casual/Unprofessional:**
❌ "Lit" / "Fire" / "Slay"
❌ "Fam" / "Squad goals"
❌ "Vibes" / "Energy check"
❌ "Let's get this bread"

**Why:** Undermines professional credibility. We're tech professionals, not influencers.

---

**Performative Diversity Language:**
❌ "Celebrating diversity and inclusion"
❌ "Diverse voices at the table"
❌ "Underrepresented minority community"
❌ "Inclusive excellence framework"

**Why:** Sounds like corporate HR theater. We're authentic community, not checking DEI boxes.

---

**Transactional Networking Speak:**
❌ "Expand your network"
❌ "Elevator pitch opportunity"
❌ "Strategic connections"
❌ "Business card exchange"

**Why:** Implies transactional relationships. We build authentic community, not LinkedIn add counts.

---

**Victim Framing:**
❌ "Underrepresented and marginalized"
❌ "Struggling to break barriers"
❌ "Seeking inclusion in tech"
❌ "Hoping for a seat at the table"

**Why:** Implies passivity and victimhood. We frame from strength: "We're building the future of tech. Join us."

## Communication Principles

### 1. Clarity Over Cleverness

**Principle:** Information should be immediately understandable. Don't sacrifice clarity for wordplay or cleverness.

**Examples:**

✅ **On-Brand:**
- "Third Thursday of every month, 6-9 PM at [Venue]"
- "Register here: [link]"
- "Free parking in the Stonewall Lot"

❌ **Off-Brand:**
- "Mark your calendars for our monthly tech fiesta!" (what day? what time?)
- "Discover your next career catalyst at our gathering" (what is this event?)

---

### 2. Belonging Before Business

**Principle:** Lead with community and belonging. Job opportunities and professional value are results, not the pitch.

**Examples:**

✅ **On-Brand:**
- "Charlotte's Latino tech professionals gathering monthly to build community, share experiences, and grow careers together."
- "Find your people. Build your network. Advance your career."

❌ **Off-Brand:**
- "Professional networking event for career advancement and opportunity access"
- "Maximize your tech career trajectory through strategic connections"

---

### 3. Code-Switching is Strategy

**Principle:** Mixing English and Spanish is intentional, not careless. It mirrors how our audience naturally communicates and signals cultural belonging without requiring fluency.

**Examples:**

✅ **On-Brand:**
- Subject line: "Nos vemos Thursday! Cafecito Tech Jan 2026"
- Post caption: "Three new jobs this month—¡éxito to Carlos, Maria, and Diego! 🎉"
- Event reminder: "See you tomorrow at Cafecito Tech. Don't forget to bring business cards (or just bring yourself—nos conocemos igual)."

❌ **Off-Brand:**
- Full Spanish: "¡Únete a nuestra comunidad este jueves!" (excludes English-dominant attendees)
- Zero Spanish: "Join our community this Thursday!" (misses cultural connection)

**Data:** Bilingual subject lines get 18% higher open rates. Code-switching captions generate 2.8x higher engagement.

---

### 4. Honesty About Challenges

**Principle:** Acknowledge real pain points without dwelling in negativity. Frame from solution and strength, not victimhood.

**Examples:**

✅ **On-Brand:**
- "52% of Latino tech workers report feeling isolated at work. Cafecito Tech exists to end that isolation."
- "Imposter syndrome is real, especially when you're the only one in the room who looks like you. Let's talk about how to push through it."
- "Breaking into tech is hard. Breaking in as a Latino professional comes with extra obstacles. Here's how others did it."

❌ **Off-Brand:**
- "Latinos are underrepresented and marginalized in tech. Come commiserate with us." (victim framing)
- "Everything is amazing! No problems here!" (toxic positivity, dismissive)

---

### 5. Consistency in Structure

**Principle:** Predictable format reduces friction. Use consistent communication templates for recurring messages.

**Event Announcement Template (4 weeks out):**
```
Join us for Cafecito Tech!

📅 [Date - Third Thursday]
🕕 6:00-9:00 PM
📍 [Venue Name + Address]
🎤 Speaker: [Name, Title] on [Topic]

This month: [Brief description of theme/content]

Your people are here. Nos vemos Thursday!

Register: [link]
```

**Event Reminder (48 hours out):**
```
Cafecito Tech is this Thursday!

Quick details:
• Doors open at 6 PM
• Parking: [specific lot/street]
• Dinner provided (vegetarian options available)
• Bring: Yourself + business cards (optional)

See you at [Venue]. Nos vemos!
```

**Post-Event Follow-Up (24 hours after):**
```
Gracias for joining Cafecito Tech!

📸 Photos: [link to gallery]
💬 Join the conversation: [WhatsApp group link]
💼 Job opportunities from sponsors: [link]

Next event: [Date] - mark your calendar!

Tu comunidad tech en Charlotte.
```

## Voice in Different Contexts

**Purpose:** Drive event registration, share professional content, amplify community wins

**Tone:** Professional, data-backed, confidence, clear CTAs

**Format Preference:** Carousel posts (3-5x higher engagement)

**Example:**
```
📊 Charlotte has 85,000 tech workers.
🧑‍💻 ~3,000-6,000 are Latino.
🤝 Zero events serve this community.

Until now.

Cafecito Tech launches Feb 28. Monthly gatherings for Latino tech professionals to build community, share experiences, and advance careers.

Third Thursday, every month. Your people are here.

Register: [link]

#CafecitoTech #LatinosInTech #CharlotteTech
```

## AI System Integration Guidelines

### For AI Content Creators (Avatar Use)

**When generating LinkedIn posts:**
- Lead with value or insight
- Use carousel format preference
- Include 1 Spanish phrase maximum
- Always include clear CTA (register, join, DM)
- Hashtags: 5-10 mix (#CafecitoTech + #LatinosInTech + #CharlotteTech)

**When generating Instagram captions:**
- Lead with emotion or visual description
- Maximum 2 Spanish phrases
- Emoji use: Minimal, intentional (☕🤝🎉 acceptable; avoid overuse)
- User-generated content preferred (4x engagement boost)
- Always tag location: @cafecitotechclt

**When generating WhatsApp/Community messages:**
- Conversational, brief
- Spanish use: Natural code-switching
- Quick value (job post, intro, meetup invite)
- Sign with name (personal touch)

### Voice Consistency Checks

**Before publishing, verify:**
1. ✅ Does this sound like a knowledgeable friend, not a corporation?
2. ✅ Would a bilingual professional say this naturally?
3. ✅ Is essential information in English (accessibility)?
4. ✅ Does Spanish enhance cultural warmth without excluding?
5. ✅ Is this confident without being arrogant?
6. ✅ Does this build belonging before business?
